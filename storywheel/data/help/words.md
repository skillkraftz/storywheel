# Words
An offline dictionary and thesaurus, and tools for finding the word you want: Lookup, Suggestions, Vocabulary, Story words and Genre words. Words works from every mode, including the Writer, which hands over the word under the cursor.
## Keys
### WordsScreen | Everywhere in Words
filter: Filter the lists in Lookup (or search, in the lists that have a search box)
add: Learn this word: the one you are on, on any tab (it becomes ★ in Vocabulary)
use: Use in Writer: go back and replace the word you were on, in the same form (only when you came from the Writer)
back: Back to the previous word you looked up
forward: Forward to the next word you looked up
copy: Copy the word
wordlist: Use in this universe's stories: pick the slot (job, thing, place...) and the Wheel and Builder will use the word
known: Mark the word you are on ✓ Known (never offered again), on any tab
learning: Mark the word ★ Learning
remove: Remove a ★ word (or a word you added to a universe list)
flashcards: Practise the ★ words with flashcards (space shows the meaning)
entity: Add the word to the universe, or make it a character, place or thing
spell: Add the word to the spelling list
rename: Rename the word everywhere in the story (with a preview)
more: Invent more names like these, in the genres' style
## Mouse
- Click a word in any list to select it; Enter or a double click looks it up or opens it. The wheel scrolls each box on its own.
## Lookup
Look any word up: meanings, similar and opposite words, rhymes and related words, each in its own box. Type a word and press Enter. Plurals, past tenses and misspellings work. The answer is in five boxes: Meanings (by part of speech), Similar, Opposites, Rhymes (perfect rhymes first, then near rhymes, grouped by syllables; the first box at its top limits the syllables, the second adds names and rare words) and Related (wider, narrower, parts, forms). Each scrolls on its own; on a narrow screen they are tabs. Enter or a click on a word in any box looks it up, and c, a, w and the buttons work on the word you are on, in any box. Rhymes come from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, fetched by storywheel dictionary install.
## Suggestions
Words to try in your story: ones that fit its genres and are not used yet, ones the dictionary relates to your characters and places, and fresh alternatives for what you overuse. The first box picks the list; the second the part of speech. The actions are Genre words': Enter looks a word up, l marks it ★ Learning, c copies, u uses it in the Writer, w puts it on a generator list.
## Vocabulary
Words worth learning, a fresh batch at a time: mark them ★ Learning or ✓ Known, practise the ★ ones with flashcards, or type a word of your own. Pick how rare, the part of speech and the subject, then New batch (Start over forgets what you have seen). Difficulty: uncommon (like "lantern"), rare (like "serendipity"), very rare (like "gallivant"). A word that appears in any manuscript of any universe is a word you know: it is marked ✓ Known automatically and leaves the lists.
## Story words
The names and odd words your story really uses, with counts and where, look-alikes flagged, and its most often used words. Pick a story (or the whole universe) and it is read at once. ◆ is a name from your universe, ? a word the dictionary does not know, ≈ a look-alike ("Glasswater" / "Glass Water"). Enter shows where each is; Enter on a place opens the Writer there. s adds the word to the spelling list, e makes it a character, place or thing, r renames it everywhere (with a preview). The second box switches to Often used: the most frequent words (everyday words left out) and words repeated close together.
## Genre words
Long lists of nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs, ranked by how well they fit a genre, plus a few of the Wheel's own lists to borrow from. Genres… ranks the words by how well they fit one or more genres: the best fits come first, ●●● ●●○ ●○○ show the fit, and nothing is hidden. The other boxes pick how common a word is, the order (genre fit, commonness, A to Z) and a search that filters as you type. From the Wheel: e adds a name, place or thing to the universe, m invents new names in the genres' style.
