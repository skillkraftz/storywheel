# Screenplays
A story whose format is screenplay is written as a script: one Fountain file (manuscript/script.fountain), plain text that any screenwriting app can open. Choose Screenplay (feature film) or Screenplay (short film) as the format: in the Wheel's structure step (click the format line) or on the Builder's story form (+ Story, or m on a story to change one). The structure list then offers the screen structures, and the target is in pages. The prose rules never apply to a script: its blank lines mean something, so they are kept, pasted text keeps its layout, there is no paragraph indent and no scene-break rewriting.
## Starting a script
P in the Builder (on a screenplay story) writes script.fountain from the outline: a title page, then each beat as a Fountain section (# Act One, ## Sequence A) with the beat's text as a synopsis (= ...). Sections and synopses never print; write the scenes under them. An existing script with scenes is never replaced. A script you start in the Writer gets a title page and FADE IN:.
## Writing Fountain in the Writer
A scene heading is a line on its own starting INT., EXT., EST. or I/E. (or any line starting with a dot), with a blank line before and after. A character cue is an uppercase name after a blank line, with the speech straight under it; a parenthetical is a line in brackets inside a speech. A transition is an uppercase line ending in TO: (or any line starting with >). > THE END < is centered. === starts a new page. ~ starts a lyric. *italic*, **bold** and _underline_ work as in prose.
Tab cycles the line you are on: action, character, parenthetical, dialogue, transition. Each step fixes the blank lines around the line so Fountain reads it that way, and an empty line becomes a character cue.
Enter after a cue or a parenthetical starts the dialogue on the next line; Enter after dialogue, action, a heading or a transition leaves a blank line and starts the next element. In the middle of a line Enter just splits it.
Scene headings are capitalized when you leave them; a cue is capitalized when you press Enter on a name the script or the universe already knows.
At a cue, the names of the script's characters and the universe's characters complete; after INT. or EXT., locations from the script and the universe's places.
On screen the page is approximated: cues, parentheticals and dialogue are indented and transitions pushed right, with display-only indents. The file stays plain Fountain.
The sidebar lists the scenes by heading, grouped under their sections (acts and sequences); Enter jumps to a scene, a adds one, r renames its heading. Move a scene by cutting and pasting it.
## A new name typed in lowercase
In Fountain a character cue is an uppercase line with the speech straight under it. A name typed in lowercase (mara, then Enter, then words) is a line of action that happens to be a name, and Fountain reads it that way. The Writer can't tell a new character from the start of a sentence, so it leaves it as action rather than guess and change what you wrote. A name the script or your universe already knows is capitalized on Enter. For a new one, type it in capitals, or press Tab on the line to make it a cue.
## The page estimate
The status line shows the estimated length against the target: p. 12 of ~15. It wraps every line at the PDF's widths and allows a little for what moves at page bottoms, so it runs within a fraction of a page of the real PDF on a short script. The target is the story's target length in pages, set on the story form (m in the Builder; a feature film starts at 110, a short film at 12). The flip test compares the same estimate with the target.
## (CONT'D)
When a speech breaks across a page, the PDF always ends the page with (MORE) and starts the next with NAME (CONT'D). When a character speaks again after a line of action in the same scene, the PDF adds (CONT'D) after the name too: Final Draft and Fade In both do this by default. Settings > Export > Screenplays: automatic (CONT'D) turns it off, for writers who prefer the cleaner page. In the Writer it shows dimmed after the name; the file keeps just the name, so turning it off later leaves nothing to clean up. A cue you wrote with (CONT'D) yourself never gets a second one. Final Draft and Fade In add their own to the .fdx when they open it, by their own setting.
## The flip test
Alt+F (Settings > Keys) runs the flip test: what a reader notices flipping through the pages. It lists action blocks longer than four printed lines, speeches longer than ten, camera directions in a spec script (we see, CAMERA, ANGLE ON, PAN, ZOOM...), CUT TO: used more than once every five scenes, and the length against the target when it is more than 15% off. Enter on a line jumps there. It never runs on its own.
## Export
From the Builder (x) or the Writer's menu: a script exports as a PDF in standard screenplay pages (US Letter, 12-point Courier Prime, 1.5 inch left margin, cues at 3.7 inches, dialogue from 2.5 to 6 inches, page numbers from page 2, (MORE) and (CONT'D) when a speech breaks across a page, never a scene heading alone at a page bottom), as Final Draft (.fdx) or as Fountain (.fountain). The title page comes from Settings > You: the title centered with Written by and your name, your contact block bottom left. An anonymous export leaves your name and contact out. Exports go in the usual manuscripts folder and are recorded like every other export.
## A last check
storywheel's PDF follows the common spec-script standard and the tests read it back. Before a big submission (a contest, an agent) opening the .fdx or .fountain in a dedicated app such as Fade In or Final Draft and looking at the pages there is optional insurance: those apps have decades of edge cases behind them.
