# Writing prose
A short story or a novel is written as prose: one file, manuscript/manuscript.md (a novel has one file per chapter), plain text with a little markup. This is the guide to how the Writer treats prose; the Screenplays page covers scripts.
## Paragraphs
Enter starts a new paragraph on the next line: one line is one paragraph, and each is shown with an indent (on screen only; the file has no spaces in front). Blank lines are allowed and mean nothing. Pasting removes leading spaces and empty lines, so pasted text follows the same rule. Alt+J joins a selection into one paragraph.
## Scene breaks
A scene break is *** on a line of its own (typing *** and Enter works too; the Keys tab has a key that inserts one). *** Title names the scene that starts there. The export prints a centered # for each break.
## Italic and bold
*italic* and **bold** (the keys are on the Keys tab). The marks are hidden on screen and the text shows in italic or bold; the export keeps them.
## Names
Tab completes names: type three letters of any word of a character's or place's name, any case. A peek key shows the character or place under the cursor (Keys tab).
## Centered lines
A line centered with the center key (Keys tab) is stored as >text< and shown centered. The .docx, .odt and .pdf exports center it; .txt centers it in plain text and .md uses a centered block. The markers are not counted as words.
## Spelling marks
Red wavy means not a word; blue a lowercase letter where a capital belongs; pink a rare word; cyan another region's spelling. Settings > Spelling softens or hides the last three. Quotes stay straight in the file; the export makes them curly.
## The scene sidebar
The sidebar key (see the Keys tab): Enter jumps, a adds a scene, r renames, J and K move it down and up, q closes.
## Words and goals
The status line shows the words in this scene, in the story against its target length (set on the story form: m in the Builder), and written today against your daily goal.
